# Central Auto Repair Website Discovery and Build Plan

Research date: August 31, 2026

Status: Pre-build discovery; no website has been implemented or published

Primary location: 82 Lincoln Hwy, Edison, NJ 08820

## 1. Executive recommendation

Build a fast, mobile-first local-service website that makes three actions effortless:

1. Call the shop.
2. Get directions.
3. Request service without implying that the request is a confirmed appointment.

The strongest positioning opportunity is not “we do everything.” Nearly every nearby shop says that. Central should instead compete on clarity, proximity, real local proof, and a clearly explained TPMS programming service.

Recommended positioning direction, pending client approval:

> **Straight answers. Reliable repairs. Back on the road.**
>
> Local auto repair in Edison for maintenance, diagnostics, tires, brakes, and TPMS programming.

The visual direction should borrow the strongest idea from the former Clutch Performance site—cinematic, real automotive photography—but use a much clearer mobile conversion path than Clutch or the nearby competitors.

## 3. Service scope

The client stated that Central provides essentially the same repair categories as B-C Tire, with three explicit exclusions and one important addition.

### Include in the initial website scope

- Oil changes and routine maintenance
- Engine diagnostics and repair
- Brake service and repair
- Tire services, including rotation and balancing
- Steering and suspension repair
- Exhaust system repair
- Cooling system repair
- Electrical system repair
- Heating and air-conditioning/climate-control repair
- **TPMS sensor programming and relearn**

These categories align with the featured services shown on the [B-C Tire homepage][bc-home], except wheel alignment, which the client explicitly excludes.

### Explicitly exclude

- Wheel alignment
- Auto glass repair or replacement
- WeatherTech products

Do not show those services in navigation, cards, metadata, structured data, or “near me” SEO copy. Do not use “complete auto care” language if it would imply services the shop does not offer.

### Confirm before publishing any claim

- Exact weekly hours and holiday-hours process
- Canonical business name, address format, and phone number
- Whether walk-ins are accepted
- Whether a service request is an appointment request or a confirmed booking
- Tire sales, brands, used tires, mounting, balancing, rotations, and flat repair
- TPMS capabilities: programming, relearn, diagnosis, sensor replacement, supported makes, and customer-supplied sensors
- Transmission work
- Fleet service
- NJ inspection or emissions service
- Towing or roadside assistance
- Warranty terms
- ASE, NAPA, CARFAX, AAA, or other certifications/affiliations
- Financing, coupons, or payment methods
- After-hours key drop, shuttle, pickup/drop-off, or waiting-room amenities
- Languages the staff can reliably support

### Market conclusion

The market divides into three groups:

1. **Strong local reputation, weak website:** B-C, B & H, and several Facebook-only shops.
2. **Modern template, generic message:** A1st, Auto Motion, and similar independents.
3. **High-friction digital leaders:** chains and aggressive local sites with online booking, coupons, sticky overlays, or large review counts.

Central can occupy a useful middle ground: polished enough to inspire confidence, simple enough to use while standing beside a disabled vehicle, and personal enough to feel different from a chain.

### Recovered design characteristics

- Single-page, full-screen section navigation
- Dark photographic background with a black overlay and white typography
- Slideshow using branded Toyota Supra photography from the shop
- Prominent logo panel and icon-based left navigation on desktop
- Sections for home, about, services, contact information, and message form
- Minimal black-and-white interface with an immersive performance-shop mood
- Open Sans/Asap-era typography and a “coming soon” template foundation

### What to preserve as inspiration

- Real, dramatic vehicle and shop photography
- Strong contrast
- Automotive energy without cartoonish icons
- A concise, confident tone
- A visually memorable first screen

### What not to copy

- The logo, photos, wording, code, or branded Supra assets
- Full-screen scroll-jacking
- Icon-only navigation without clear labels
- A hero without immediate call/directions actions
- Long-running background slideshows or video on mobile
- A single-page structure that makes service-specific search visibility difficult

Recommended translation: **Clutch's cinematic attitude with Central's service clarity.**

## 6. Differentiation strategy

### Primary position: clarity over “one-stop shop” sameness

“One stop auto repair,” “expert technicians,” “quality service,” and “honest pricing” appear throughout the category. They are table stakes, not differentiators.

Central should demonstrate clarity through the experience:

- Put the phone number, address, directions, and open/closed status first.
- Explain the service process in three steps.
- Use real photos of the building, people, bays, and tools.
- Publish only specific credentials and warranty terms the client can prove.
- Make the request form short and explain when the customer should expect a response.

### Signature service: TPMS programming

TPMS is valuable but not unique—Mavis actively advertises TPMS service locally, and chains also appear for the search. Central's opportunity is specificity.

Create a dedicated TPMS page that answers:

- Is the warning light solid or flashing?
- Does Central diagnose, program, relearn, replace, or all four?
- Can the shop work with customer-supplied sensors?
- Which makes/models and sensor types are supported?
- How long does a typical programming/relearn visit take?
- Does programming require an appointment?

Suggested page title after verification:

> TPMS Sensor Programming & Relearn in Edison, NJ

Do not promise universal compatibility, a fixed time, or a fixed price until the shop confirms them.

### Local proof instead of stock imagery

Most nearby sites use generic mechanics, generic vehicles, or template layouts. A modest set of strong original images will differentiate Central more than a complex animation system:

- Exterior/signage from Route 27/Lincoln Highway
- Owner and technicians, with permission
- Clean service bays
- TPMS programming tool in use
- Brake/tire/diagnostic work
- Customer handoff or front desk

### Reputation gap as the highest-priority business issue

The website cannot by itself offset a profile with one Google review when immediate competitors have 50–1,391. A compliant review-generation process should launch alongside the site:

- Give every completed-service customer the direct Google review link.
- Use a printed counter card or receipt QR code.
- Ask consistently; never gate, script, buy, or incentivize positive reviews.
- Respond to reviews in the owner's real voice.
- Show current reviews on the website only through a maintainable source or manually approved excerpts.

## 7. Recommended MVP information architecture

Keep the first release small and useful.

1. **Home** — positioning, core services, trust, process, TPMS feature, reviews, location, and CTA
2. **Services** — complete approved service list with short customer-focused explanations
3. **TPMS Programming** — dedicated search and education page
4. **About** — real team/shop story and approved credentials
5. **Contact / Request Service** — call, directions, hours, short form, and response expectations
6. **Privacy** — required if a form, analytics, or embedded third-party services collect data

Do not add a blog, tire e-commerce catalog, login, financing application, coupons engine, or live scheduling system to the MVP unless the client has a real operating process for it.

## 8. Homepage content plan

### 1. Mobile utility header

- Logo/business name
- Click-to-call phone
- Menu button with text alternative
- Optional small “Open today” status only after hours are verified and implemented accurately

### 2. Hero

Recommended draft, pending client approval:

- Eyebrow: `LOCAL AUTO REPAIR · EDISON, NJ`
- Headline: `Straight answers. Reliable repairs. Back on the road.`
- Subheadline: `From oil changes and brakes to diagnostics, tire service, and TPMS programming, Central Auto Repair helps keep your vehicle safe and dependable.`
- Primary CTA: `Call (732) 822-4534`
- Secondary CTA: `Get Directions`
- Supporting fact: `82 Lincoln Hwy, Edison, NJ`

Use one excellent real shop or technician image, not a carousel.

### 3. Symptom-led service entry

Help customers recognize their problem before presenting technical categories:

- Warning light on
- Brake noise or vibration
- Tire pressure light
- Engine running rough
- Vehicle overheating
- Heat or AC not working

Each item should lead to the relevant service section or call action.

### 4. Core service grid

Show the approved ten categories. On mobile, use a single-column or compact two-column layout with readable descriptions. Do not hide the entire list in a carousel.

### 5. TPMS feature band

Give TPMS its own visual treatment and link to the dedicated page. Use a photo of the actual programming tool or sensor, not a generic tire-pressure icon alone.

### 6. Three-step plan

1. **Tell us what's happening** — call or send a service request.
2. **Get an inspection and explanation** — the shop identifies the issue and explains the recommended work.
3. **Approve the repair** — work begins only after the customer approves the scope.

The wording in steps 2 and 3 must be confirmed as the shop's actual practice.

### 7. Why Central

Use verified proof only. Candidate proof slots:

- Years in business
- Technician certifications
- Warranty
- Local ownership
- Same-day work
- Supported vehicle types
- Actual Google/CARFAX review excerpts

If a proof point is unavailable, leave it out rather than filling the space with “quality, integrity, excellence.”

### 8. Reviews

At launch, do not build a large review carousel around one Google review. Show a modest Google rating/link or approved CARFAX excerpts with platform attribution. Expand after the review profile grows.

### 9. Location and final CTA

- Canonical name, address, and phone
- Verified weekly hours
- “Get Directions” link that opens Google Maps
- Lightweight static map image or lazy-loaded embed
- Short service-request form
- Clear emergency disclaimer if the form is not monitored continuously

### 10. Mobile sticky action bar

Use two or three actions maximum:

- Call
- Directions
- Request Service

It must not cover page content, form controls, cookie notices, or accessibility tools.

## 9. Visual direction

### Mood

- Confident, practical, local, and mechanically precise
- Dark charcoal or ink for depth
- Warm off-white for readable content surfaces
- One restrained accent derived from the actual signage/logo—not B-C's palette
- Steel-gray supporting tones

### Typography

- Strong condensed or industrial display face for major headings
- Highly legible sans serif for body, hours, forms, and service explanations
- Avoid ultra-condensed body copy and all-caps paragraphs

### Image treatment

- Real photography first
- Slightly cinematic crops and controlled dark overlays in the hero
- Natural color elsewhere so the shop looks trustworthy and real
- No generic AI mechanics, exotic cars unrelated to the customer base, or competitor assets

### Avoid

- Autoplay video on mobile
- Full-screen sliders
- Multiple overlapping sticky widgets
- A coupon banner before the shop identity
- Stock-photo-heavy service cards
- Glassmorphism, arbitrary gradients, or decorative animation on every section
- Horizontal overflow at 320–430 px widths

## 10. Mobile and accessibility requirements

- Design mobile-first at 320, 360, 390, and 430 px widths before desktop expansion.
- Keep primary buttons at least 44 × 44 px with comfortable spacing.
- Keep body text at a practical mobile reading size; do not shrink important address or hours text.
- Meet at least 4.5:1 contrast for normal text and 3:1 for large text, controls, and meaningful graphics.
- Include a visible focus indicator on every interactive control.
- Use semantic landmarks and a logical heading hierarchy with one descriptive H1 per page.
- Make the entire navigation keyboard operable; Escape closes the mobile menu.
- Label icon-only controls for assistive technology.
- Supply useful alt text for real shop/service images and empty alt text for decoration.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` and avoid persistent movement.
- Never rely on color alone to communicate status or service category.
- Ensure the sticky action bar does not trap focus or obscure the last content/form field.

## 11. Local SEO and trust implementation

### On-site

- Unique title and description for each MVP page
- One canonical business name/address/phone everywhere
- Descriptive service copy written for customers, not keyword repetition
- `AutoRepair`/local business structured data based only on verified facts
- Embedded or linked Google map
- Internal links from symptoms to services and from services to the request action
- Optimized real images with descriptive filenames and alt text
- Fast static rendering and lazy-loaded below-the-fold media

### Google Business Profile, after owner approval

- Add the launched website URL
- Reconcile the name, address, phone, and hours
- Add every genuinely offered service, especially TPMS programming
- Upload current exterior, interior, team, and service photos
- Add appointment/service-request link only if its workflow is monitored
- Establish a repeatable review-request process

The live profile should not be changed during design/build without business-owner approval.

## 12. Technical recommendation

The workspace is currently empty and is not a Git repository.

Preferred implementation for this scope:

- Static, component-based site such as Astro, with TypeScript where useful
- Semantic HTML and custom responsive CSS; no heavy UI framework is required
- No database for the MVP
- Serverless or hosted form handler only after the destination email, spam controls, consent language, and operational owner are confirmed
- Environment variables for any service keys; never commit secrets
- Image optimization during build
- Deployment preview before domain or Google Business Profile changes

If the team wants the fewest possible moving parts, a well-structured static HTML/CSS/JavaScript build is also viable. The decision should follow whoever will maintain the site, not trend preference.

## 13. Suggested implementation sequence

### Phase 0 — facts and assets

- Resolve canonical business identity and phone
- Confirm hours and complete service matrix
- Confirm TPMS capabilities
- Collect logo/signage and original photos
- Confirm credentials, warranty, amenities, and languages
- Decide how service requests are received and answered

### Phase 1 — content and low-fidelity review

- Approve positioning and homepage copy
- Approve sitemap and mobile content order
- Produce a simple 390 px wireframe
- Confirm all CTA destinations

### Phase 2 — visual system and build

- Create tokens for color, typography, spacing, radius, focus, and states
- Build mobile components first
- Expand to tablet and desktop
- Add metadata and structured data from verified facts

### Phase 3 — verification

- Test at 320, 360, 390, 430, 768, 1024, and desktop widths
- Test call, directions, menu, form, and every internal link
- Test keyboard navigation, focus, contrast, alt text, and reduced motion
- Run mobile performance/accessibility checks
- Verify no excluded service appears in copy or metadata
- Obtain client approval on a private preview

### Phase 4 — launch, only after approval

- Connect the approved domain and hosting
- Verify HTTPS and redirects
- Submit the sitemap to search tools
- Add the website/service-request URL to Google Business Profile
- Recheck canonical business information and form delivery in production
- Begin the review-generation process

## 14. MVP acceptance criteria

- A new customer can identify the shop, location, and service scope within five seconds on a phone.
- Call and directions actions are visible in the initial mobile viewport.
- TPMS programming is prominent and has a dedicated explanatory page.
- Wheel alignment, auto glass, and WeatherTech are absent from public service claims.
- The site contains no unverified credentials, awards, warranties, review totals, or timing promises.
- No horizontal scrolling or content-obscuring overlays occur at target widths.
- All core actions work with touch, mouse, and keyboard.
- The form clearly states that submission is a request, not a confirmed appointment.
- The site passes the agreed performance and accessibility review before launch.
- Canonical name, address, phone, and hours match the owner-approved Google Business Profile data.
