Steering and suspension · Edison, NJ

How the car tracks and settles tells a story.

A clunk over bumps, extra bounce, steering looseness, or a pull can come from overlapping tire, wheel, steering, suspension, or brake conditions. Note the road, speed, direction, and corner where it feels strongest.

Ask about steeringService guides

What the driver notices

See, hear, smell, or feel something different?

These details help identify a useful starting point. They are not a diagnosis, and the same symptom can come from more than one condition.

SEE

Uneven stance or tire wear

Compare wheel openings and tread patterns without crawling under the vehicle.

HEAR

Clunk, knock, or squeak

Note whether it happens over bumps, while turning, braking, or entering a driveway.

SMELL

Hot tire or brake odor

An odor near one corner may be related or separate; report it with the handling change.

FEEL

Pull, wander, bounce, or looseness

Describe the speed, road surface, steering input, and whether braking changes the feeling.

Plain-language system view

Control and comfort share connected parts.

Steering parts translate wheel input into direction. Suspension components support vehicle weight, locate the wheels, and manage movement over the road. Ball joints, tie rods, shocks, struts, and sway-bar links each play different roles but can create overlapping symptoms when worn or damaged.

A pull does not automatically mean alignment, and alignment is not listed in Central’s current published services. Tire pressure and wear, brakes, road crown, steering, suspension, and other conditions can influence tracking. An inspection starts with the complete symptom.

Before you call

Have these details ready.

Location and timing help turn a vague clunk into useful evidence.

  1. 01

    Year, make, model, mileage, and which corner feels or sounds different

  2. 02

    Road type, speed, turn direction, braking, or bump that triggers the concern

  3. 03

    Photos of uneven tire wear or visible stance differences taken from a safe position

  4. 04

    Recent tire, wheel, brake, steering, suspension, or collision-related work

Safety first

Sudden loss of steering control is an emergency.

If steering becomes very loose or binds, a wheel appears out of position, the vehicle pulls sharply, a component is dragging, or control feels unsafe, stop driving in a safe place and seek assistance.

Common questions

A clearer starting point.

Does pulling mean I need an alignment?

Not always. Tire, brake, road, steering, and suspension conditions can all affect tracking. Alignment is not listed in Central’s current catalog, so call to discuss the symptom and available inspection work.

What is the difference between shocks and struts?

Both help control vehicle movement, but their construction and role differ by vehicle. The year, model, and symptom determine which components are present and relevant.

Can worn suspension affect tire wear?

It can be one factor among several. Bring photos and note which edge or tire is wearing so the vehicle can be evaluated in context.
